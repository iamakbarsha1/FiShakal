# FiShakal — Food, Prices, Swaps

Prices are approximate Chennai rates (Koyambedu / local market / Aavin booth, late 2026). Supermarket rates run 15–25% higher. Check your own market — treat these as ratios, not exact figures.

## 1. Protein per rupee — the table that decides your budget

This is the whole budget problem in one table. Protein is the expensive macro; everything else is cheap.

**What this means in practice:** soya chunks, dal, chana and eggs carry your protein. Chicken, fish and beef are the flavour and the micronutrients, 3–4 days a week, not daily. That one decision is the difference between ₹4,000 and ₹8,000 a month.

Boneless chicken breast is cheaper per gram of protein than whole chicken (no bone waste) — if your shop sells it at ₹300/kg, it's actually the better buy despite the higher sticker price.

## 2. Soya chunks — read this once

Cheapest protein available in India by a wide margin, and most people cook it badly.

- 100 g dry = ~52 g protein = roughly the protein in 7 eggs, for ₹33.
- **Prep:** boil 5 minutes in salted water → drain → **squeeze out the water hard** → then cook. Skipping the squeeze is why it tastes like a wet sponge.
- Soaks up any masala. Works in sundal, in kuzhambu, as a chicken-curry extender (half chicken, half soya — nobody notices), in biryani.
- 50 g dry is one good portion. It triples in size.
- Reasonable daily, not unlimited. 50–100 g dry/day is fine.

## 4. Ingredient upgrades — what to buy first as budget grows

In order of value per rupee, best first:

1. **More fish, not more chicken.** Sardine and mackerel give omega-3 that chicken doesn't. ₹150/kg. Biggest health return per rupee on this list.
2. **Liver once a week.** ₹150/month. Covers your main micronutrient gaps.
3. **More vegetables, especially keerai.** ₹10–15 a bundle. Fibre and micronutrients, nearly free calories.
4. **Whole fruit instead of nothing.** Guava (high fibre, high vitamin C, ₹60/kg) and papaya (₹40/kg) beat apples and imported fruit on value.
5. **Almonds / walnuts** (₹700–900/kg) — only after the above. Good fats, but peanuts at ₹120/kg do most of the same job.
6. **Whey protein** (₹2,000/kg) — convenience only. Buy it if you're genuinely missing protein, not because it's "fitness food".
7. **Greek yogurt / hung curd** — you can make this at home by straining normal curd through a cloth for 2 hours. Doubles the protein per spoon. Costs nothing extra.
8. **Vanjaram / seer fish, prawns, mutton** — tastes great, poor value per rupee. Treat food, not plan food.

**Don't buy, at any budget:** protein bars, diet biscuits, "sugar-free" sweets, multigrain bread (it's white bread with colouring), fat burners, green tea extract, apple cider vinegar, detox anything, atta noodles, "healthy" breakfast cereals.

## 5. Cheap vegetables that stay cheap in Chennai

Year-round and under ₹60/kg: cabbage, cucumber (vellarikkai), pumpkin (parangikkai), ash gourd, bottle gourd (suraikkai), ridge gourd (peerkangai), brinjal, radish, banana stem (vaazhaithandu), raw papaya, all keerai varieties (arai, mulai, siru, ponnanganni) at ₹10–15 a bundle.

Build your poriyal and salad around these. Carrot, beans and capsicum are fine but cost 2–3× more for no real nutritional advantage.

## 6. Cooking rules that save calories without changing the dish

- **Oil:** measure it. 1 tablespoon = 120 kcal. A family kuzhambu can carry 4–5 tablespoons. Ladle your portion from the top, above the oil layer.
- **Coconut:** flavour is in the first 2 tablespoons. Grated coconut is ~350 kcal per 100 g. Reduce, don't remove.
- **Fry vs curry:** chicken 65, fish fry, bajji — frying adds 100–200 kcal per portion. Grill, bake, tawa-fry with ½ tsp oil, or air-fry if you have one.
- **Rice:** cook it, cool it, reheat it — cooling converts some starch to resistant starch, which cuts the usable calories slightly and feeds gut bacteria. Small effect, free to do.
- **Curd over coconut chutney** with idli/dosa when you can — fewer calories, more protein.
- **Salt:** keep it normal. Low-salt diets aren't part of this plan; you have no blood pressure issue.

## 7. Eating outside — the decision list

| Order this                                        | Avoid this                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Grilled chicken / mandi (eat meat, half the rice) | Biryani with extra ghee rice                 |
| Idli, plain dosa                                  | Parotta, bun parotta, ghee roast             |
| Shawarma, no mayo                                 | Shawarma with mayo + fries                   |
| Chicken 65 → ask for grilled / tandoori instead   | Anything fried                               |
| Rasam, sambar, curd rice                          | Sweet lassi, badam milk, falooda             |
| Black tea/coffee, lime soda (no sugar)            | Sugarcane juice, soft drinks, packaged juice |
| Egg dishes, fish curry meals                      | Bakery: puffs, roast, cake, halwa            |

**Dawat / wedding / family function:** eat normally and enjoy it. One large meal does not undo a week. What undoes a week is deciding the whole plan is ruined and eating badly for the next four days. Eat the biryani, take protein and salad alongside, go back to normal at the next meal.
